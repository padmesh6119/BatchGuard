# BatchGuard project direction

Keep user responses extremely short, readable, and direct. Default to one-line updates and a few final bullets. Expand only when requested.

The product is one inspector decision screen: which medicine batches to sample first within this week's capacity. Combine Layer A (real CDSCO history, July 2025–August 2026) and Layer B (clearly labeled simulated verification scans). Preserve both layers; do not substitute a historical-only review product or make hypothetical flooding the central objective.

Dashboard target: persistent real/simulated banner; one ranked row per batch; separate historical, scan-anomaly, and evidence-quality values; priority and reasons; capacity and measured coverage; arriving simulated scans; batch evidence with timeline, districts, source counts, and manufacturer history; evaluation and measured laptop/LinuxONE results.

The roughly 10% finding refers to earlier manufacturer-text recurrence under a specified split. Report the actual split and measured value; do not treat it as a universal prediction ceiling or proof of scan-detector effectiveness.

Primary deliverables: working combined demo, reproducible code/notebook, fair baseline comparisons, actual IBM Z measurements, honesty slide, and demo video. Winning IBM Z Datathon 2026 is the user's objective. Prioritize substantive measured improvements.

Prepare portable training inputs and reproducible commands now. Once assigned LinuxONE access is available, prioritize actual training/inference execution, compatibility fixes, and matched workload measurements there. Hardware acceleration must be measured, not assumed.

Training targets and feature/split contracts are documented in BatchGuard/MODEL_FOUNDATION.md. Layer A labels next-month report recurrence, not safe/unsafe medicine. Layer B labels simulated clone behavior. Preserve held-out splits and keep identifiers/scenario names out of features.

Humans decide. Never automatically block medicine or declare counterfeiting from an anomaly. Claim only measured results; distinguish real data, simulations, and actual IBM Z execution. Trained models must be evaluated against baselines. Hypothetical attack hardening is deferred unless requested.
